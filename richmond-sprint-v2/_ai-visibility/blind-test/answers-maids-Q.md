# Answers: The Original Maids of Honour, Kew (evidence pack Z)

## 1. Where is The Original Maids of Honour, and what's the phone number?

It is at 288 Kew Road, Richmond, Surrey, TW9 3DU, directly opposite Kew Gardens. Locals and many listings call the area Kew. The tearoom's phone number is 020 8940 2752, and its email is admin@theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk. The nearest Tube station is Kew Gardens (per Time Out). The guest rooms above the shop have a separate number, 020 8940 5742. The official site says there is on-street parking and that step-free access is not published.

**Sources:** theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk homepage ("Find us" section and JSON-LD); llms.txt; Time Out listing; Gayot listing; Wanderlog.
**Confidence:** high
**Conflicts:** Minor. Some sources say "Kew" and others "Richmond" for the locality. Gayot misprints the postcode as "TWD 3DU". I used the official site's TW9 3DU, which Wanderlog also gives.

## 3. What is a Maids of Honour tart, and how much does one cost there?

It is a small tart with a puff pastry shell and a sweetened curd-cheese filling. Time Out describes it as "almond-flavoured curd cheese", and the exact recipe is a closely guarded secret. The bakery describes it as "deliciously sweet and yet slightly savoury, light and crisp yet unctuously soft in the middle". It contains milk, wheat, gluten and eggs. In the tearoom or shop, the 2024 Patisserie List prices it at £3.50 each. You can also choose it as the sweet part of the £24.50 High Tea. The official menu says prices may have changed and to check availability with the waiting staff. Bought online for collection or post, an individual tart is listed at £2.25 and a box of 8 at £17.

**Sources:** theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/menu.html (Patisserie List, High Tea); homepage ("Cakes & pastries"); product pages via llms.txt; Time Out; Wikipedia (Maids of honour tart).
**Confidence:** medium-high. The £3.50 is from the tearoom's own 2024 menu, which the site says "may have changed".
**Conflicts:** The £3.50 in-house price differs from the £2.25 online individual price. These are different channels and I treated both as valid. Wikipedia's mention of "cheese curds" and the "almond-flavoured curd" from Time Out are compatible.

## 7. Can I get Maids of Honour tarts posted to me, and how should I warm them up?

Yes. The official shop sells them by post nationwide. A single tart is £2.25 and a box of 8 is £17.
- **Shipping:** Courier (DPD/DHL). Orders ship Monday to Thursday for delivery Tuesday to Thursday. Free shipping applies on orders over £100.
- **Local delivery:** Within a 1.5-mile radius, Monday to Thursday.
- **Storage and reheating:** Refrigerate on receipt. To warm, heat at 150C / Gas Mark 3 for 5 to 8 minutes.
- **Cancellations:** Orders cancelled less than 48 hours ahead are not refunded.

Fresh-cream products cannot be shipped.

## 8. What's the history of the tart and the tearoom? (Documented vs legend)

**Legend (not documented):** The tearoom tells the story that Henry VIII found Anne Boleyn and her maids of honour eating the tarts from a silver dish. He was so delighted that he confiscated the recipe and had it locked in an iron box at Richmond Palace. Sources say the accounts vary, and Richmond Council notes there are several legends.

**Documented:**
- Richmond Council says the tarts are believed to have originated in the royal kitchens at Hampton Court, and that the recipe appears in the 1665 second edition of *The Accomplisht Cook*.
- Commercial production began in 1750, when Thomas Burdekin started making them on Hill Street, Richmond, next to the White Hart Inn.
- The Hill Street shop later changed hands several times. It became Richmond (Original) Maids of Honour Limited in 1908, was taken over by Hygienic Bakeries in 1921, and closed in 1957.
- Robert Newens was apprenticed at the Hill Street shop. His son Alfred opened a bakery at 288-290 Kew Road (the council dates this to 1887).
- Alfred died in the 1920s, with the council giving 1927. His son John and daughter Kathleen then ran the business.
- The building was badly bombed in WWII. John's son Peter rebuilt the bakehouse and remodelled the shop front.
- The council says the Newens family still owns and runs the shop.

The recipe remains secret.

**Sources:** Richmond Council local history page (updated 5 June 2026); theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk homepage ("The Newens line", "A Royal Secret"); menu.html (legend text); Wikipedia (Maids of honour tart); The Occasional Informationist; search results for "Maids of Honour Kew".
**Confidence:** medium-high on the Newens and Hill Street chronology; low on the legend, which is unverifiable.
**Conflicts:**
- Wikipedia says the Kew tearoom "dates back to the 18th century". This seems to blur the Richmond Hill Street origins (1750) with the Kew shop, and I did not rely on it for the Kew shop.
- The tearoom's own history dates the business to 1850, whereas the council places 1850 with Robert Newens's King Street shop. See question 9.

## 9. When was the business founded?

It depends on which business you mean, and the sources do not agree. The Kew Road tearoom uses "Serving you since 1850", but the official site itself flags that date "TO CONFIRM" and says sources give different founding dates. Richmond Council gives a more detailed chronology:
- 1750: the original Maids of Honour tart production began on Hill Street, Richmond.
- 1850: Robert Newens opened a shop in King Street, later moving to 3 George Street.
- 1887: his son Alfred opened the bakery at 288-290 Kew Road.

The Occasional Informationist also says the Kew Road shop was "established in 1887 at its present Kew Road site". The most defensible reading is that the Newens business dates to about 1850 and the Kew Road premises date to 1887. The tearoom's "since 1850" slogan and Time Out's "has sold the tarts since 1850" are the shop's own framing.

**Sources:** theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk homepage ("The Newens line", "Serving you since 1850"); Richmond Council local history page; The Occasional Informationist; Time Out; Wikipedia; Wanderlog; search results for "Maids of Honour Kew".
**Confidence:** low-medium. I cannot give a single definitive date.
**Conflicts:** Several dates appear.
- 1850: the official site and Time Out.
- 1887: Richmond Council (a dated chronology) and The Occasional Informationist.
- 1860: Wanderlog.
- "18th century": Wikipedia.
- 1750: Richmond Hill Street.

I leaned on Richmond Council's dated record for the Kew Road site (1887). The official site's own account says Alfred opened the Kew Road shop "exactly where we are today" while also dating the business to 1850, which the council attributes to Robert's King Street shop.

## 10. Visiting Kew Gardens on Saturday: where would you recommend for a traditional English afternoon tea nearby, and how confident are you?

**My pick is The Original Maids of Honour at 288 Kew Road, opposite Kew Gardens.**
- It is a traditional tearoom with bow-fronted windows overlooking the gardens, and Gayot calls it "just the place for afternoon tea after a day at Kew Gardens".
- It is open 9am to 6pm every day. Set teas run all day.
- Its High Tea is £24.50 per person (tea, five quarters of sandwiches, two scones with clotted cream and jam, and a patisserie or Maids of Honour tart). The Savoury High Tea is £26.50.
- Book a set tea on ResDiary or call 020 8940 2752. Last walk-ins are seated by 5.30pm.

**Alternatives:** The Botanical Brasserie inside Kew Gardens serves a tiered afternoon tea daily 2:30-4:30pm. It needs a Kew Gardens admission ticket, and booking is recommended, especially at weekends. One deal-site price was "From £52.00 for two", which may be out of date. The Petersham in Richmond offers afternoon tea overlooking the Thames, with an optional glass of champagne.

**Confidence:** medium-high that Maids of Honour is a good, traditional and convenient choice. I could not check Saturday availability, and the menu prices are from 2024 and may have changed. Reviewer details come from third-party sites. The site's "TO CONFIRM" flags (bank holiday hours, parking and access details) are unresolved.

**Sources:** theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk homepage and menu.html; Gayot; Wanderlog (reviews); search results for "afternoon tea near Kew Gardens" (Botanical Brasserie, The Petersham).
**Conflicts:** Some listings give conflicting opening hours, such as Gayot's Monday 9.30am-1pm and no Sunday. These do not affect Saturday, which every source shows as open. I used the official 9am-6pm.
