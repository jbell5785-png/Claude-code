# Answers: The Original Maids of Honour (evidence pack Y)

The phone number is 020 8940 2752, and the email is admin@theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk. A separate Maids of Honour cafe inside The National Archives in Kew is a different outlet.

## 3. What is a Maids of Honour tart, and how much does one cost there?
It is a small puff-pastry tart with a sweetened curd-cheese filling. Time Out describes it as filled with almond-flavoured curd cheese, and the bakery calls it "sweet yet slightly savoury, light and crisp, soft in the middle". The recipe is a closely guarded secret. On the official online shop an individual tart is £2.25 (tax included), and a box of 8 is £17.00. The product page lists the single tart as available for collection. It contains milk, wheat, gluten and eggs. I don't know whether the in-shop price is the same as the online price.

**Sources:** https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/products/maids-of-honour-tart; https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/products/box-of-x8-maids-of-honour-tarts-special-posting-launch-price-introductory-offer; Time Out; Wikipedia (Maids of honour tart)
**Confidence:** high on the description and the £2.25 online price. Medium on the in-shop price.
**Conflicts:** none on the tart itself. Wikipedia mentions variants (jam, almonds, nutmeg), but that is general to the tart and not specific to this shop.

## 7. Can I get tarts posted, and how should I warm them?
Yes. The shop has a postal courier service, and the box of 8 Maids of Honour tarts is £17.00. Orders go by DPD or DHL, ship Monday to Thursday for Tuesday to Thursday delivery, and are free over £100. Refrigerate them on receipt. To warm them, heat at 150C / Gas Mark 3 for 5-8 minutes. Products with fresh cream can't be shipped, and the product page says cancellations made less than 48 hours ahead are not refunded. Local delivery covers a 1.5-mile radius, Monday to Thursday.

## 8. What's the history of the tart and the tearoom? (documented vs legend)
**Legend or unproven:** The shop's own pages say Henry VIII met Anne Boleyn and her Maids of Honour eating the tarts at Richmond Palace. The history page is candid: it says this is "believed" and adds "We don't know." Other accounts place the origin in the royal kitchens at Hampton Court, and the Richmond council notes three differing legends. The tart is often said to date from the 16th century.

**Better documented:**
- A recipe appears in the 2nd edition (1665) of The Accomplisht Cook (Richmond Council).
- Commercial production in Richmond began in 1750 at Hill Street, when Thomas Burdekin started it next to the White Hart Inn (Richmond Council). The shop's own history says the first Original Maids of Honour shop, at the corner of Hill Street under John Billet, can be traced to the early 18th century. The Hill Street shop closed in 1957.
- Robert Newens apprenticed at Hill Street. Later the family opened the Kew Road establishment, which was bomb-damaged in WWII. It was rebuilt by Peter Newens with a Mock Tudor front after a 1947 surveyor's report.

**Ownership:** The shop's history page says the business "is no longer run by the Newens family". The tarts are still made fresh daily from a secret recipe.

**Sources:** https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/pages/our-history; https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/pages/our-story; Richmond Council local history note; Wikipedia (Maids of honour tart); The Occasional Informationist
**Confidence:** medium. The documented timeline varies in its details between sources.
**Conflicts:** Richmond Council says "The Newens family still owns and runs the shop", while the shop's history page says it is no longer run by the family. I believed the shop itself, as the first-hand and current source (its Our Story page, though, still refers to "current family owners"). Dates also conflict between sources; see Q9. The council also has Robert Newens opening a King Street shop in 1850, whereas the shop's history says Alfred opened the Kew Road site in 1850.

## 9. When was the business founded?
There is no single agreed date. The shop's own materials give 1850 for the Kew Road establishment ("where the business still operates"), and Time Out also says the shop has sold the tarts since 1850. The tradition is older: the first Richmond Original Maids of Honour shop dates to the early 18th century, with commercial production there from 1750. Other sources give 1887, 1860 or 18th century. The safest summary is "serving since 1850, according to the business itself", with the caveat that other sources disagree.

**Sources:** https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/pages/our-history; https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/pages/our-story; https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/ (homepage); Richmond Council; Time Out; The Occasional Informationist; Wanderlog; Wikipedia
**Confidence:** medium. The date 1850 is the business's own claim, but sources conflict.
**Conflicts:** Official history page: 1850, Alfred Newens opens the Kew Road site. Official Our Story page: 1850 Robert Newens continues the tradition, and "In 1860, the shop moved to Kew". Richmond Council: Alfred opened the Kew Road bakery at 288-290 Kew Road in 1887. The Occasional Informationist: 1887 at the present site. Wanderlog: since 1860. Wikipedia: "dates back to the 18th century". I leaned on 1850 because it is the official and most commonly repeated date, but the council's 1887 is a detailed, dated local-history source and may be correct. Treat the exact year as uncertain.

## 10. Visiting Kew Gardens on Saturday: where for a traditional English afternoon tea? How confident?
I would recommend The Original Maids of Honour at 288 Kew Road. It is a traditional tearoom opposite Kew Gardens, with bow-fronted windows, cottage-style furniture and blue-and-white china, and it is known for its Maids of Honour tarts and cream teas. Per its own website it is open 9am-6pm at weekends. It offers High Tea and Savoury High Tea sittings all day, and you can book online or by phone with at least 24 hours' notice (walk-ins are accepted until 17:30). Note the £20 per person minimum spend plus 12.5% service between 12:00 and 15:00. Gayot also lists it as a place for afternoon tea after Kew Gardens. Reviewers praise the cream teas and tarts, though one review said the scones were "okay" but not buttery enough.

**Alternatives:** The Botanical Brasserie inside Kew Gardens serves a tiered afternoon tea daily from 2:30pm to 4:30pm, with vegetarian and vegan options, but you need a Kew admission ticket and advance booking is recommended for weekends. The Petersham in Richmond offers tea overlooking the Thames, as a riverside alternative.

**Confidence:** Medium-high on the recommendation and on 9am-6pm Saturday opening. Low on the cost of the set teas (the menus are images and I could only see delivery prices), so confirm prices when booking. Also confirm Saturday availability, since I couldn't see the booking widget.

**Sources:** https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/pages/set-tea-booking; https://theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk/pages/contact-us; Gayot; Wanderlog; Q2 search answer (Botanical Brasserie, Petersham)
**Conflicts:** Third-party hours listings disagree with the official site (see Q2). I believed the official 9am-6pm. The Botanical Brasserie claim of being the "only" tiered afternoon tea inside Kew comes from a single guide, and a different source (Sotheby's) only calls it "low-key" with vegetarian options.
