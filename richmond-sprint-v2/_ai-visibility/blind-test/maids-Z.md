"The Maids of Honour is a tea room and bakery on Kew Road, opposite Kew Gardens, and the tart it is known for has a contested origin story.
- The shop: Reviewers describe it as a traditional tea room with bow-fronted windows overlooking the gardens. Its listed hours are 9.30am-6pm Tuesday to Saturday, with shorter hours on Monday. A travel blog notes that the waiting staff wear traditional black and white dress. The address is 288 Kew Road, TW9 3DU, with the Kew Gardens Tube station as the nearest transport.
- History: Sources disagree on the founding date. One says the business developed from an earlier one in Richmond and was established in 1887 at its present Kew Road site. Another says the Kew Road shop has been selling them since 1850. The Richmond tradition is older still: commercial production of the tarts in Richmond began in 1750 on Hill Street.
- The tart: It is a puff pastry shell with a sweetened curd filling. The recipe is secret, but reportedly involves curd cheese. The origin story links it to Henry VIII, but the accounts vary. One legend says Henry VIII presented the recipe to Anne Boleyn, a lady-in-waiting. Richmond's own council notes that the tarts are believed to have originated in the royal kitchens at Hampton Court."

During World War II, “the elegant early Victorian building that housed the bakery, shop and dining room suffered severe bomb damage…”

“John Newens’ son Peter left the army and with his family, set work to get the business back on its feet. The bake house was rebuilt with new gas ovens installed, and the shop front was remodelled.”

“Visitors today to the Original Maids of Honour are greeted by this very same shop front…”

The Newens line
Robert Newens “served an apprenticeship” at the first Original Maids of Honour shop, on the corner of Hill Street in Richmond, “and went on to open his own premises, first in King Street and later at No 3 George Street…”
Alfred Nashbar Newens, his son, “opened a brand new establishment on the Kew Road – exactly where we are today.” The business dates it to 1850. TO CONFIRM
John and Kathleen: “Alfred Newens died, leaving his business to his son John and daughter Kathleen in the 1920s.”
Peter Newens, John’s son, rebuilt the bake house and remodelled the shop front after the war.

A Royal Secret

Legend has it that Henry VIII came across Anne Boleyn and her Maids of Honour “eating the tarts from a silver dish. Tasting one for himself, the King was so delighted by its ‘melt-in-the-mouth’ sensation that he confiscated the recipe and demanded it be kept secret in a locked iron box at Richmond Palace.”

Hill Street: 1750 Thomas Burdekin began commercial production next to the White Hart Inn; 1790 sold to William Hester; later John Lea, John Thomas Billett (from 1830); 1908 Richmond (Original) Maids of Honour Limited; 1921 Hygienic Bakeries; 1957 Hill Street shop closed. Newens: Robert Newens apprenticed at Hill Street; "1850: He opened a shop in King Street, later moving to number 3 George Street"; "1887: His son Alfred opened a bakery at 288-290 Kew Road, Kew"; 1927 Alfred died; WWII bomb damage; Peter rebuilt. "Today: The Newens family still owns and runs the shop."
