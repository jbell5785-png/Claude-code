# Answers: The Original Maids of Honour, Kew (evidence pack X)

## 1. Where is The Original Maids of Honour, and what's the phone number?

It is at 288 Kew Road, Richmond, Surrey, TW9 3DU, directly opposite Kew Gardens. The nearest Tube station is Kew Gardens. The phone number is 020 8940 2752, and the email is admin@theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk. The shop's own site gives "Richmond, Surrey" as the postal locality, while other sources call it "Kew". They mean the same address. Parking is on-street only, and the site doesn't say whether access is step-free.

**Sources:** theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk (homepage, "Find us" and JSON-LD); llms.txt; Time Out listing; Wanderlog; Gayot.
**Confidence:** high
**Conflicts:** Gayot prints the postcode as "TWD 3DU", which is clearly a typo. I used TW9 3DU, which the official site, Time Out and Wanderlog all give. The "Kew" versus "Richmond" naming is not a real disagreement.

## 2. What are its opening hours?

The tearoom's own site says the shop and tearoom are open every day, Monday to Sunday, 9am to 6pm. Within that, breakfast runs from 9am (cooked and toasted items last orders at 11am). Luncheon runs 12 noon to 3pm. Set teas run from 10am, with Prosecco and Champagne teas from 11am. Walk-ins must be seated by 5.30pm. The site says bank holiday and Christmas hours are not published, so call 020 8940 2752 for those.

Some third-party listings differ (see Conflicts), so I'd trust the official 9-6 hours but phone to confirm if you're travelling a long way.

**Sources:** theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk homepage ("Find us", JSON-LD openingHoursSpecification); llms.txt; Wanderlog (daily 9AM-6PM).
**Confidence:** medium-high. The official site is clear, but it marks parts of the schedule "TO CONFIRM" and gives no bank holiday hours.
**Conflicts:** Time Out gives 8.30am-6pm daily, but that is an old listing (titled 2016, page dated 2019). Gayot gives Monday 9.30am-1pm, Tuesday to Saturday 9.30am-6pm, and no Sunday. Dishcult (known only from search text) gives 9am-6pm. One search summary said Monday has shorter hours. I believed the official site, backed by Wanderlog, because it is the business's own current statement and the third-party listings are older or inconsistent with each other. The National Archives café is a separate site with different hours and is not this shop.

## 3. What is a Maids of Honour tart, and how much does one cost there?

It is a small puff-pastry tart with a sweetened curd-cheese filling. Time Out describes it as "almond-flavoured curd cheese", and the recipe is a closely guarded secret. The tearoom describes it as "deliciously sweet and yet slightly savoury, light and crisp yet unctuously soft in the middle". It contains milk, wheat, gluten and eggs. On the tearoom's menu one tart costs £3.50. That comes from the tearoom's 2024 menus, and the site warns prices may have changed. Availability should be checked with the waiting staff. A tart can also be chosen as part of the High Tea.

**Sources:** theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk (menu.html, Patisserie List; homepage); Wikipedia "Maids of honour tart"; Time Out.
**Confidence:** high for the description, medium for the price (it is from 2024 menus).
**Conflicts:** none on the description. The £3.50 price is the only figure given for in-shop purchase, and I found no conflicting price. Deliveroo prices for other items differ slightly from the in-shop menu in places, but no Deliveroo price for a single tart was available.

## 8. What's the history of the tart and the tearoom? (Documented vs legend)

**Legend:** The best-known story is that Henry VIII found Anne Boleyn and her maids of honour eating the tarts from a silver dish. He supposedly loved them so much that he confiscated the recipe and had it locked in an iron box at Richmond Palace. The tearoom tells this story itself and says the recipe is still secret. Richmond Council records three legends and says the tarts are believed to have originated in the royal kitchens at Hampton Court.

**Documented:** Richmond Council's local history notes say a recipe appears in the 2nd edition (1665) of *The Accomplisht Cook*. They say commercial production began in 1750 at Hill Street, Richmond, when Thomas Burdekin started making them next to the White Hart Inn. The Hill Street business passed through several owners and closed in 1957. Robert Newens apprenticed at Hill Street and opened his own shop (King Street, later 3 George Street). His son Alfred opened a bakery at 288-290 Kew Road in 1887, and Alfred died in 1927. The Kew Road building was badly bomb-damaged in WWII and was rebuilt and remodelled by Alfred's grandson Peter Newens. The Council says the Newens family still owns and runs the shop.

**Sources:** Richmond Council local history page (updated 5 June 2026); theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk homepage ("A Royal Secret", "The Newens line"); Wikipedia; The Occasional Informationist.
**Confidence:** medium-high for the documented chain, low for the legend (it is unverifiable by nature and the accounts vary).
**Conflicts:** The shop's own site dates its Kew Road shop to 1850, while the Council says 1850 was Robert Newens opening in King Street and 1887 was Alfred's Kew Road bakery. Wikipedia says the tearoom "dates back to the 18th century", which seems to conflate it with the 1750 Richmond origin. I believed the Council, as the most detailed dated source.

## 9. When was the business founded?

It depends on what you count. The tearoom itself says "Serving you since 1850" and Time Out says the shop has sold the tarts since 1850, but the tearoom's own site marks this "TO CONFIRM" and acknowledges that sources give different founding dates. Richmond Council's history dates the Kew Road bakery to 1887, when Alfred Newens opened it. It dates 1850 to his father Robert opening a shop in King Street. The Occasional Informationist also says 1887 for the present Kew Road site. The original Richmond tart business (Hill Street) dates to 1750. So 1887 is the best-supported date for the Kew Road shop, and 1850 is the shop's own marketing date.

**Sources:** Richmond Council local history page; theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk (footer, "The Newens line"); Time Out; The Occasional Informationist; Wanderlog; Wikipedia.
**Confidence:** medium. The sources really do disagree.
**Conflicts:** 1850 (the tearoom's sign-off, Time Out), 1860 (Wanderlog, "since 1860"), 1887 (Council, Occasional Informationist), "18th century" (Wikipedia) and 1750 (Hill Street origin). I favoured 1887 for the Kew Road site because the Council and the tearoom's own Newens history (Alfred opening on Kew Road) are consistent with it, and the 1850 date appears tied to Robert's earlier shop. The tearoom flags its own 1850 claim as unconfirmed.

## 10. I'm visiting Kew Gardens on Saturday and want a traditional English afternoon tea nearby. Where would you recommend, and why? How confident are you?

I'd recommend The Original Maids of Honour at 288 Kew Road, opposite Kew Gardens. It is a traditional tearoom and bakery, and it is open every day 9am to 6pm. Set teas run all day (High Tea £24.50, Savoury High Tea £26.50, Special Set Tea £50, per the 2024 menu), and you can book a set tea on ResDiary or just walk in until 5.30pm. Gayot calls it "just the place for afternoon tea after a day at Kew Gardens", and reviewers praise the tarts and cream teas. An alternative is the Botanical Brasserie inside Kew Gardens, which offers a tiered afternoon tea daily from 2:30 to 4:30pm. It needs a valid Kew admission ticket, and booking ahead is advised for weekends. A deal site listed it from £52 for two, which may be out of date. For a riverside setting, The Petersham in Richmond serves afternoon tea overlooking the Thames. I'm fairly confident about the Maids of Honour (official hours, menu and booking), but prices are from 2024 and may have changed. Saturday is the busiest day, so booking is wise. I haven't checked current availability, and I have no first-hand review of the other venues.

**Sources:** theoriginalmaidsofhonour.co.uk (homepage, menu.html, llms.txt); Gayot; Q2 search results for Botanical Brasserie and The Petersham; Wanderlog.
**Confidence:** medium-high for Maids of Honour, medium for the alternatives.
**Conflicts:** Gayot's older hours listing shows Tuesday-Saturday 9.30am-6pm, which still covers Saturday afternoon. The official site (9am-6pm daily) is consistent for Saturday. The Botanical Brasserie price and hours come from guide and deal-site text and are less certain. Another search answer described it as a "low-key choice" without prices.
