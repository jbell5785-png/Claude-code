"The Maids of Honour is a tea room and bakery on Kew Road, opposite Kew Gardens, and the tart it is known for has a contested origin story.
- The shop: Reviewers describe it as a traditional tea room with bow-fronted windows overlooking the gardens. Its listed hours are 9.30am-6pm Tuesday to Saturday, with shorter hours on Monday. A travel blog notes that the waiting staff wear traditional black and white dress. The address is 288 Kew Road, TW9 3DU, with the Kew Gardens Tube station as the nearest transport.
- History: Sources disagree on the founding date. One says the business developed from an earlier one in Richmond and was established in 1887 at its present Kew Road site. Another says the Kew Road shop has been selling them since 1850. The Richmond tradition is older still: commercial production of the tarts in Richmond began in 1750 on Hill Street.
- The tart: It is a puff pastry shell with a sweetened curd filling. The recipe is secret, but reportedly involves curd cheese. The origin story links it to Henry VIII, but the accounts vary. One legend says Henry VIII presented the recipe to Anne Boleyn, a lady-in-waiting. Richmond's own council notes that the tarts are believed to have originated in the royal kitchens at Hampton Court."

### Richmond Council https://www.richmond.gov.uk/services/libraries/branch_libraries/local_studies_collection/local_history_notes/richmond_maids_of_honour (updated 5 June 2026)
Origins: Hampton Court royal kitchens; three legends; recipe in 2nd edition (1665) of The Accomplisht Cook. Hill Street: 1750 Thomas Burdekin began commercial production next to the White Hart Inn; 1790 sold to William Hester; later John Lea, John Thomas Billett (from 1830); 1908 Richmond (Original) Maids of Honour Limited; 1921 Hygienic Bakeries; 1957 Hill Street shop closed. Newens: Robert Newens apprenticed at Hill Street; "1850: He opened a shop in King Street, later moving to number 3 George Street"; "1887: His son Alfred opened a bakery at 288-290 Kew Road, Kew"; 1927 Alfred died; WWII bomb damage; Peter rebuilt. "Today: The Newens family still owns and runs the shop."

### The Occasional Informationist https://theoccasionalinformationist.com/2019/04/22/kew-maids-of-honour/ (22 Apr 2019, updated 6 Oct 2024)
Developed from an earlier Richmond business; "established in 1887 at its present Kew Road site"; bombed in WWII, rebuilt late 1940s; customers included royalty, film stars, Winston Churchill; tart "said to date from the 16th century"; quotes the bakery's "sweet yet slightly savoury, light and crisp yet unctuously soft in the middle"; recipe reportedly includes curd cheese.
