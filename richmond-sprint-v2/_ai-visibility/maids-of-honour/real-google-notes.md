## Right
- **Hours:** Mon–Sun 9am–6pm. Set teas from 10am, or 11am for Prosecco and Champagne. Last seating 5:30pm.
- **Thursday breakfast:** no cooked breakfast on Thursdays, with bakery pastries still available. This comes from their breakfast booking page, which is HTML text.
- **Set-tea prices:** High Tea £24.50, Savoury High Tea £26.50, Prosecco from £35, Champagne from £45, Special Set Tea £50. But see the problem below.
- **Tarts:** £3.50 in the tearoom; online £2.25 each, £17 for a box of 8, £25.50 for a box of 12; postal courier service.
- **Contact:** phone 020 8940 2752; walk-ins accepted.

## Wrong or shaky
- **Set-tea prices come from the gift-voucher page.** Google says the dine-in options "are represented by their set voucher pricing". The real tearoom menus are photos it can't read, so it guesses from the voucher shop. It happens to match this time; it will drift as soon as menu prices change.
- **Lunch rule applied to afternoon tea.** Answering "how much is afternoon tea… and how do I book?", Google says "If you dine between 12:00 and 15:00, a strict minimum spend of £20 per person applies".
  - That is the Luncheon rule, presented as an afternoon-tea condition. Our blind test's "today" AI made exactly the same mistake.
  - The business's own pages also disagree: £20 on the page, £22 on the printed menu.
- **Founding history scrambled into a false timeline.** "Founded in 1850 by Robert Newens… 1860: the shop moved to a location in Kew. 1887: the business moved to its current site on Kew Road (288 Kew Road)."
  - The business says *Alfred* opened on the Kew Road in 1850, "exactly where we are today". Robert was the apprentice who opened in King Street and George Street.
  - The 1860 and 1887 dates come from other sources (their Our Story page and the Council). Google stitched three conflicting dates into one confident timeline.
  - Google's own business profile says "since 1860", while the logo says Est. 1850.
- **"Chintzy family-run"** (Google profile). The business's own history page says it is no longer run by the Newens family, while the Council says the family still owns it. To confirm with the owner.
- **Unverified stockist:** "Forman & Field occasionally stock them (around £6)".
- **Competitors:** for traditional afternoon tea, the AI also pushes Pembroke Lodge ("Georgian mansion nestled right inside Richmond Park"), citing Reddit and others.

## Pitch takeaway
AI gets the basics right where Maids publishes text (hours, the Thursday rule, tart prices). It goes wrong exactly where the content is photos or conflicting pages:
- It prices the teas from a voucher shop.
- It applies the lunch minimum spend to afternoon tea.
- It invents a founding timeline.

The redesign puts every menu in text, separates the rules for each service, and states one clear history line ("Est. 1850, the business's own date") with the legend labelled as legend.
