## The place, in one paragraph
A café in "converted arches beneath Richmond Bridge", on the towpath at Richmond.
- **The bridge:** Grade I listed, 1777, by Paine and Couse, and the oldest bridge over the Thames in Greater London. Its five Portland stone arches rise to the widest and tallest at the centre.
- **The arch:** brick, with a carved bearded stone face as its keystone over a glazed arched screen.
- **Inside:** a long whitewashed brick vault with ferns hanging in baskets, strings of lights, red enamel shades, grey polka-dot oilcloths, mismatched wooden chairs, and a long counter with chalkboards (one headed "Organic Wines & Beers").
- **The garden:** gravel under three great plane trees, beside the bridge's stone arches.
- **The river:** tidal here. Richmond's half-tide weir (opened 1894) raises its gates about two hours either side of high water. Next door, a boat-hire board reads "HIGH WATER · Ride the tide · LEAVE before H.W.".
- **The café's voice:** "Great coffee, great music, great food… The sort of place you can hang out all day for the price of a cup of coffee." It is organic and Fairtrade.
- **The sister café:** Hollyhock, up in Terrace Gardens, reached from the towpath via Grotto Gate. "Stop in and ask for a free local map!"

## Three directions

### A: "Under the keystone" (the vault)
- **Only-here details:** the brick arch with its carved bearded keystone face; the whitewashed barrel vault; ferns in hanging baskets; festoon lights following the curve; polka-dot oilcloths; mismatched chairs; chalkboards; the far door at the end of the tunnel.
- **Idea:** you enter the website through the arch. Every page lives inside the vault: whitewash, soft brick showing through, lights strung along the curve.
- **Signature, "Through the arch":**
  - The hero is an original, finely drawn SVG of the arch front: brick voussoirs, the carved face at the keystone (drawn with character), the glazed screen and the hanging "CAFÉ" sign.
  - On scroll, the view passes under the keystone and down the length of the vault in perspective. Ferns sway gently, the festoon bulbs light along the curve, and the tunnel ends at the bright far door, which opens onto the garden and the river.
  - Keep it short, with no scroll-jacking. Reduced motion shows the composed vault as a still.
- **Menu (sample layout):** chalkboards on the vault wall. Draw your own chalk lettering style and render the type with an SVG chalk texture, so it reads like real chalk and stays legible (AA). The banner states it's a sample.
- **Textures:** your own polka-dot oilcloth pattern for section backgrounds, and whitewash with brick showing through. Sparingly.

### B: "Five arches" (London's oldest Thames bridge)
- **Only-here details:** Richmond Bridge (Grade I, 1777, Paine and Couse, the oldest Thames bridge in Greater London); its five Portland stone arches rising to the tallest at the centre; the café inside its arches; "toll-free since 1859" (see identity #15); the plane trees beside the stone.
- **Idea:** an engraver's elevation of the bridge is the site. Elegant, 18th-century and architectural: copperplate line drawing on Portland-stone paper.
- **Signature, "The five arches":**

  - On mobile, the arches become a swipeable or condensed bridge bar.

### C: "Ride the tide" (the river at the door)
- **Only-here details:** the tidal Thames at Richmond; the half-tide weir (1894), whose gates rise about two hours either side of high water; the "HIGH WATER · Ride the tide · LEAVE before H.W." boat-hire board; the riverside steps and wooden skiffs; the gravel garden under three plane trees; the towpath tunnel and the walk to Hollyhock through Grotto Gate, using the café's own "free local map" offer.
- **Idea:** the river is the clock. A bright, outdoorsy, contemporary site that rises and falls like the water at its door.
- **Signature, "The waterline":**
  - A drawn waterline runs across the whole site, over the riverside steps illustration. It rises and falls with scroll, like the tide on the Richmond steps.
  - A small panel explains the half-tide weir in one sentence of verified fact, and links to the Port of London Authority's official tide tables for real times.
