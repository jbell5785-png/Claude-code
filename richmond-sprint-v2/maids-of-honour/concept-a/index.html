Kew Road

Our story: the very same shop front

During World War II, “the elegant early Victorian building that housed the bakery, shop and dining room suffered severe bomb damage…”

“John Newens’ son Peter left the army and with his family, set work to get the business back on its feet. The bake house was rebuilt with new gas ovens installed, and the shop front was remodelled.”

The new premises had “a welcoming and homely appeal with its distinctive Mock Tudor mix of painted pebbledash, red clay roof tiles, heavy timbering and casement windows”.

“Visitors today to the Original Maids of Honour are greeted by this very same shop front…”

The Newens line

  1. Robert Newens “served an apprenticeship” at the first Original Maids of Honour shop, on the corner of Hill Street in Richmond, “and went on to open his own premises, first in King Street and later at No 3 George Street…”
  2. Alfred Nashbar Newens, his son, “opened a brand new establishment on the Kew Road – exactly where we are today.” The business dates it to 1850. to confirm
  3. John and Kathleen: “Alfred Newens died, leaving his business to his son John and daughter Kathleen in the 1920s.”
  4. Peter Newens, John’s son, rebuilt the bake house and remodelled the shop front after the war.

Legend

A Royal Secret

Legend has it that Henry VIII came across Anne Boleyn and her Maids of Honour “eating the tarts from a silver dish. Tasting one for himself, the King was so delighted by its ‘melt-in-the-mouth’ sensation that he confiscated the recipe and demanded it be kept secret in a locked iron box at Richmond Palace.”

And the recipe today? “That’s still a secret – one that’s safe with us for a good long time to come…”

Made on the premises

“Everything at the Original Maids of Honour is produced ‘in house’. We do not ‘buy in’ and ‘sell on’. The ingredients are weighed and mixed by hand, the dough kneaded and pastry rolled in the traditional way.”

“The whole process happens daily – every day, seven days a week…”
