# The Original Maids of Honour: identity research

Researched and accessed 25 October 2026. Every fact has a source code. The codes are listed in full in `dossier.md` § Sources.

Labels:
- **VERIFIED**: official source, or several sources agree.
- **REPORTED**: one source only.
- **LEGEND**: a traditional story, not historical fact.

Photo evidence:
- **Official photos** are the business's own images on its website. They were viewed for reference only and are not in the repo.
- **Client photos P1 to P4** are cited as "client-supplied Google Maps photo (third-party), viewed 25 Oct 2026". They are third-party uploads, so they are reference only and must never be reproduced or traced. The coordinator's observations are in `photo-notes.md`, which this file does not overwrite.

The brief was that nothing should be generic. Each detail below exists at 288 Kew Road and nowhere else.

## 1. Only-here details, ranked by design potential

### 1. The green fascia with gold blackletter and the raised "TEAS" gable. VERIFIED
- **Evidence:**
  - A long dark-green board along the roofline reads "The Original Maids of Honour" in gold blackletter (Old English) letters.
  - A raised central gable panel reads "TEAS".
  - It is visible in the official frontage photo on the homepage, `Photos_for_Website_outside…jpg` ([S1]).
  - It is visible in the client-supplied Google Maps photo P1 (third-party), viewed 25 Oct 2026.
  - It appears in the Richmond & Twickenham Times photo reproduced on the business blog ([S19]).
  - It is drawn into the logo ([S1], logo artwork).
  - The post-war photo on the history page ([S3], `Old_Miads_large.jpg`) shows an earlier board reading "Maids of Honour" in blackletter. So the blackletter fascia goes back at least to the post-war rebuild.
- **Why build on it:** This is the business's real signboard, and the website does not use it at all. The site is black and white with Jost and Tenor Sans type. A header or navigation that reads like the fascia ties the site directly to the building. The "TEAS" gable is a ready-made "Book a table" tab.

### 2. The pen-and-ink logo drawing of the building. VERIFIED
- **Evidence:** The logo artwork ([S1]: `MOH_Artwork_-01…png`, `MOH_Logo-03.jpg`) contains:
  - A cross-hatched drawing in black ink of the current building, seen from the corner.
  - The "TEAS / The Original Maids of Honour" roof board, the side-wall sign and the tiled canopy.
  - Small-paned bay windows and a lantern over the door, with two Victorian-style street lamps on either side.
  - A blackletter wordmark "The Original / Maids of Honour".
  - Serif small capitals reading "TEA ROOMS | BAKERY | GUEST ROOMS".
  - "Est. 1850", also in blackletter.
- The drawing is also printed at the top of "The Special Set Tea" menu ([S8]).
- **Why build on it:** It is already line art, so it can be drawn on screen, animated with hatching that fills in, or used as an outline map of the building. Its parts (gable, canopy, side sign, lamps) can label sections of the site.

### 3. The recipe "kept secret in a locked iron box at Richmond Palace". LEGEND, told by the business
- **Evidence:**
  - "…he confiscated the recipe and demanded it be kept secret in a locked iron box at Richmond Palace" ([S3]).
  - The homepage says "…kept it under lock and key at Richmond Palace!" and calls the tart "A Royal Secret...." ([S1]).
  - The box-of-8 page adds "declaring that the tarts should only be made for Royal Court" ([S15]).
  - One version says the maid who invented the tart "was imprisoned within the Palace grounds" ([S3]). Richmond Council gives the same three versions of the legend ([S21]).
  - The business says the recipe is still secret: "That's still a secret – one that's safe with us for a good long time to come…" ([S3]). The menu calls it "the best kept secret in town!" ([S8], High Tea menu).
- **Historical caution:** Richmond Council says it does not matter which legend is true. It notes that a recipe appeared in R. May's *The Accomplisht Cook*, 2nd edition, 1665 ([S21]).
- **Why build on it:** A secret kept in a locked box gives the site an "open the box" moment, and it belongs to this business only. Always label it as legend.

### 4. The tart itself. VERIFIED (look and description) / REPORTED (filling)
- **Evidence:**
  - **Look:** a round puff-pastry case with flaky, layered edges. The top is a blistered, golden-to-caramel brown. It is served on a silver cake stand with a paper doily (official photo `IMG_9604_SQ.jpg` [S1]).
  - In box photos it sits in a clear tray, and it is also shown on a blue-and-white plate ([S15] product photos).
  - **Business description:** "deliciously sweet and yet slightly savoury, light and crisp yet unctuously soft in the middle" ([S3], [S14]).
  - **Business instruction:** "'Maids of Honour are finest when warmed in a moderate oven' (150° Gas Mark 3) for 5 to 8 mins … Devour when cooling from the oven, deliciously warm" ([S14]).
  - "Contains milk, wheat, gluten and eggs." ([S14])
  - Served "warm from our ovens" every day ([S1], [S3]).
  - Prices are £2.25 in the online shop ([S14]) and £3.50 on the tearoom Patisserie List ([S8]).
- **Filling:** The business does not say what is in it, because the recipe is secret. Third-party sources:
  - Time Out: "almond-flavoured curd cheese" ([S22]). REPORTED.
  - Wikipedia: "puff pastry shell filled with cheese curds" ([S23]). REPORTED, and not specific to this shop.
- **Why build on it:** The blistered golden top and flaky round edge are the hero texture. The business's own warming instruction (150°, Gas Mark 3, 5 to 8 minutes) can become an interaction.

### 5. The 1890s photo of the A.N. Newens shop. VERIFIED (published by the business)
- **Evidence:** The homepage hero ([S1]) and the history page ([S3], `New_Old_Newens_Shop…jpg`) show the earlier Victorian Kew shop.
  - **Signage:** a fascia reading "Cook and A.N. NEWENS Confectioner", with "Confectioner" in flowing script. Next to it, "REFRESHMENT ROOMS".
  - **Window lettering:** "SOUPS CREAMS & ENTREES", "&c ON HIRE", "Bermaline BREAD", "HOVIS", "CADBURY'S CHOCOLATE / COCOA", "ALE & STOUT", "SHERRY". There is also a carved coat of arms above the fascia. What it signifies is unknown, so do not imply a Royal Warrant.
  - **People:** staff in white aprons and caps, with a pony at the right.
- **Caption** ([S17]): "Alfred Newens and his family and staff posed in front of the Kew shop. Mrs Newens stands to the left of Alfred. On his right is his son Frank, who distinguished himself as a photographer and his daughter Kathleen who with John, (holding the pony) continued in business all her life."
- **Date caution:** The homepage says "IN THE 1890S" but the blog post title says "The 1800s".
- **Why build on it:** The Victorian sign writing (gilded window lettering, a script "Confectioner") is a second source of lettering, separate from the blackletter. The photo also has named people, which suits a "who's who" interaction.

### 6. From the Blitz to Mock Tudor. VERIFIED (business and Council agree on the damage and rebuild)
- **Evidence:**
  - "the elegant early Victorian building … suffered severe bomb damage" during the Second World War ([S3]; Council agrees, [S21]).
  - A 1947 surveyor's report described the site as "little more than a pile of rubble, the baker's oven a charred and blackened hulk at its centre" ([S3]). This is REPORTED, because only the business quotes it.
  - John Newens's son Peter rebuilt it, with new gas ovens and a remodelled shop front ([S3], [S21]).
  - The result was "Mock Tudor mix of painted pebbledash, red clay roof tiles, heavy timbering and casement windows" ([S3]).
  - "Visitors today … are greeted by this very same shop front" ([S3]).
- **Why build on it:** The building looks Tudor but was built in the 1940s. That fits a business named after a Tudor legend, and the history page says so: "a far more fitting home for a tart and an odyssey of Tudor origin!" The story runs Victorian shop, rubble, rebuilt Mock Tudor, and gives a clear before-and-after.

### 7. The clay-tile canopy with gold "Newens" lettering, lantern and iron cresting. VERIFIED
- **Evidence:**
  - A sloping clay-tile canopy (pentice roof) runs across the ground floor and around the curved corner bay.
  - "Newens" is set in gold letters on the tiles, with a hanging black lantern beside it.
  - A wrought-iron balcony rail runs along the canopy top, with trailing greenery.
  - Official frontage photo ([S1]); RTT photo on [S19]; client-supplied Google Maps photo P1 (third-party), viewed 25 Oct 2026.
- **Note:** `photo-notes.md` describes the "Newens" lettering as script. In the sharper official photo and the RTT close-up it is gold **blackletter**, matching the fascia.
- The RTT close-up also shows ornamental iron cresting above the canopy, and **bull's-eye (crown-glass style) roundels** set into the small-paned windows.
- **Why build on it:** The tile pattern, the bull's-eye glass and the cresting are textures nobody else has. They work as dividers, borders and hover states.

### 8. The side-wall painted sign. VERIFIED (client photo and the logo drawing)
- **Evidence:** A painted board on the side wall reads "Newens / The Original Maids of Honour / HOME MADE BREAD, CAKES & PASTRIES / MORNING COFFEE / LUNCHEONS / TEAS". The phone number underneath is too small to read with confidence.
  - Client-supplied Google Maps photo P1 (third-party), viewed 25 Oct 2026.
  - The same sign is drawn on the right of the logo artwork ([S1]).
- **Why build on it:** The sign is already a list of the day's services. It can be shown as a live "what's being served now" board (see the signature ideas).

### 9. The bakers' delivery bikes, carts and the vintage van. VERIFIED
- **Evidence:**
  - The homepage slide headed "NEWENS BAKERS BREAD CART" ([S1]) shows two old photos. One is a carrier tricycle with a box lettered "NEWENS BAKERS" and loaves stacked on top. The other is a hand cart marked "FLOUR".
  - The slide text reads "To - Posting Pies | Sausage Rolls | Pastys | Cakes | Pastries | Confectionay" (sic). Its button reads "WE ARE STILL DELIVERING".
  - Today, a cream vintage van with "Maids of Honour" in blackletter on its side is parked beside the shop. It is in the official frontage photo ([S1]), the RTT photo ([S19]) and client photo P1 (third-party, viewed 25 Oct 2026).
  - The homepage offers a "Vintage Vehicle prop" for film shoots ([S1]).
- Local delivery: "We are delivering to a 1.5 mile radius of Maids Monday to Thursday" ([S1]).
- **Why build on it:** A delivery tracker or "order for delivery" flow can use the Newens Bakers tricycle and today's van.

### 10. Blue-and-white transferware on every table. VERIFIED
- **Evidence:**
  - Blue-and-white printed cups, saucers and plates appear in the official spread photo `display_new.jpg` ([S1]) and under the tarts in the box photo ([S15]).
  - The plates show a pastoral scene with an ornate border, which looks like Spode's "Blue Italian" (unconfirmed, TO CONFIRM).
  - Client-supplied Google Maps photos P3 (cream tea) and P4 (afternoon tea) (third-party), viewed 25 Oct 2026.
  - Undated black-and-white shop interiors on [S3] show decorative plates on a high rail, so displayed china is a long habit.
- **Why build on it:** Cobalt on white is the one strong colour in the tearoom. A plate-rim border or a cobalt accent comes from the china itself, not a stock palette.

### 11. The tearoom furniture. REPORTED for today's room (one photo); the chandeliers also appear in older official photos
- **Evidence** (client-supplied Google Maps photo P2, third-party, viewed 25 Oct 2026):
  - Wheel-back Windsor chairs, round dark-oak pedestal tables and lace doilies.
  - Floral chintz curtains with matching pelmets over big paned windows.
  - **Black wrought-iron ring chandeliers with small cream shades.**
  - A tall carved dark-wood corner cabinet with a shell-niche top holding china.
  - A paned glass partition and a dark patterned carpet.
- Ring-shaped wrought-iron chandeliers with fabric shades also hang in the undated black-and-white shop photos on [S3] (`Website_History_Pictures_Page_-01/-02.jpg`). Those photos also show patterned pelmets, so the look has lasted decades.
- The restaurant page lists a "Refreshment room, Covered Courtyard & Private Dining room" ([S5]).
- **Why build on it:** The chandelier ring, the shell niche and the chintz are all motifs drawn from the room itself.

### 12. The red room with the gilt mirror and the Cadbury's sign. REPORTED (one photo)
- **Evidence:** Client-supplied Google Maps photo P4 (third-party), viewed 25 Oct 2026, shows:
  - A deep burgundy wall with a large gilt-framed mirror and a framed print.
  - A vintage enamel "CADBURY'S CHOCOLATE" sign, seen reflected in the mirror.
  - Tables in white cloths with steel teapots, strainers and a three-tier stand.
- **Link to the 1890s photo:** that photo has "CADBURY'S CHOCOLATE / COCOA" lettered on the shop window ([S1], [S3]). Whether today's sign has any link to the old shop is unknown. Do not claim it.

### 13. The table setting. VERIFIED (several photos)
- **Evidence:**
  - Stainless-steel teapots, hot-water jugs and tea strainers, with sugar cubes in a glass dish.
  - Scones with jam and clotted cream in small pots on a silver salver with a paper doily.
  - Three-tier stands with a fixed order: sandwiches at the bottom, scones with jam and clotted cream in the middle, slices of cake on top.

  - Menus confirm "Two Scones served with Clotted Cream (or English Butter) & Preserve" and "Traditional Tea-Sandwiches (5 quarters)" ([S8]).
- **Why build on it:** Two ideas fit here. The three tiers can order the set-tea menu (bottom to top: savoury, scones, sweet), and the steel pot can show loading.

### 14. Packaging: white box, black ribbon in gold blackletter, oval label. VERIFIED (official product photo)
- **Evidence:** The box-of-8 product photos ([S15]) show:
  - A plain white card box tied with black ribbon printed in gold blackletter: "Original Maids of Honour" alternating with "Newens".
  - A black oval label with a gold border: "Newens / The Original / Maids of Honour / Shop".
  - Tarts packed in a clear moulded tray.
- The shipping page adds:
  - "Our packaging contains re-usable, cooling ice packs which can last up to 72 hours" ([S12]).
  - "Our cardboard delivery boxes are 100% recyclable" ([S12]).
- **Why build on it:** Black, gold and white is the business's real "gift" palette. The oval label is a ready-made badge for cart, checkout and gift vouchers.

### 15. The family line of bakers. VERIFIED in outline; dates conflict (see the dates table in `dossier.md`)
- Robert Newens was apprenticed at the first Maids of Honour shop on Hill Street, Richmond. He then opened shops in King Street and at No. 3 George Street ([S3], [S21]).
- His son **Alfred Nashbar Newens** opened on the Kew Road ([S3], [S21]).
- Alfred's son **John** and daughter **Kathleen** took over in the 1920s. The Council says Alfred died in 1927 ([S21]).
- John's son **Peter** rebuilt the shop after the war ([S3], [S21]).
- Alfred's son **Frank** "distinguished himself as a photographer" ([S17]).
- The site says "its no longer run by the Newens family" ([S3]). The Council note says it is "still owned and run by the Newens family" ([S21]). This conflicts, so it is TO CONFIRM.
- **Why build on it:** Five named generations from a single bakery suit a family-tree timeline. Use only the historic names.

### 16. The 1907 postcard of the Hill Street "Original Shop". VERIFIED (published by the business)
- **Evidence:** A history-page image ([S3], `CCI02062011_00005_large.jpg`) is a postcard handwritten "September 25th 1907".
  - It shows a Georgian shopfront lettered "THE ORIGINAL SHOP FOR MAIDS OF HONOUR", with "BILLETT" in the windows and "LUNCHEON & TEA ROOMS" upstairs.
  - The printed caption reads "ESTABLISHED IN RICHMOND NEARLY 200 YEARS".
  - The Council confirms the Billett family on Hill Street from 1830, and that the Hill Street shop closed in 1957 ([S21]).
- **Why build on it:** It shows the tart's history before Kew. It belongs in a "before Kew" chapter, not as a picture of this building.

## 3. Signature ideas: interactions that only make sense here

1. **"Today at Maids": the side-wall sign as a live service board.**
   - The painted sign's list (MORNING COFFEE / LUNCHEONS / TEAS) lights up against the real service times:
     - cooked breakfast last orders 11am ([S6]);
     - Luncheon 12:00 to 15:00 ([S7]);
     - set teas from 10:00, and Prosecco or Champagne teas from 11:00 ([S8]);
     - last walk-ins seated by 17:30 ([S8]);
     - no cooked breakfast on Thursdays ([S6]).
   - Tapping a line opens that menu and its ResDiary booking. This fixes the website's biggest weakness (hours spread across pages) using an object that hangs on the building.
   - Based on detail 8 (VERIFIED) and the booking-page facts.

2. **The locked iron box.**
   - An iron box (drawn in the logo's pen-and-ink style) is the site's single "secret" element. It opens to show everything about the tart (its look, the "sweet and yet slightly savoury" description, "Contains milk, wheat, gluten and eggs", the order button) **except the recipe**, which stays a locked slip: "Still a secret."
   - It must carry a small "Legend" tag.
   - Based on detail 3 (LEGEND, told in the business's own words) and detail 4.

3. **"Finest when warmed": the reheating timer.**
   - On the tart product page and the postal-order confirmation, a 5-to-8-minute timer uses the business's own instruction: "warmed in a moderate oven (150° Gas Mark 3) for 5 to 8 mins … Devour when cooling from the oven".
   - The blistered top gradually "browns" in an illustration.
   - Based on detail 4 (VERIFIED [S14]).

4. **Rubble to Mock Tudor: the frontage across time.**
   - One frontage, scrubbed through three states:
     - the 1890s A.N. Newens shop;
     - the 1947 "pile of rubble … charred and blackened hulk" (shown as text only, since no photo was found);
     - the rebuilt Mock Tudor shop with its green fascia and "TEAS" gable.
   - Use the logo's line-drawing style, or the business's own archive photos with permission.
   - Based on details 1, 5 and 6 (VERIFIED). Do not put a date on the rubble frame beyond "1947 surveyor's report".
