## 2. History, with each claim labelled

**Rule:** where dates conflict, use the wording "since the 1800s on the Kew Road" or "Est. 1850 (the business's own date)". Do not choose between 1850, 1860, 1868 and 1887 without the client's confirmation.

### The legend
- **LEGEND:** "It is believed that Henry VIII, King of England from 1509-1547, came across Anne Boleyn and her Maids of Honour (the young ladies who attended the Queen), eating the tarts from a silver dish. Tasting one for himself, the King was so delighted by its 'melt-in-the-mouth' sensation that he confiscated the recipe and demanded it be kept secret in a locked iron box at Richmond Palace." (S3)
- **LEGEND:** "One account goes even further and states that … the unfortunate Maid who invented the tart was imprisoned within the Palace grounds and ordered to produce the delectable tarts solely for King Henry and his royal household!" (S3; the Council gives the same versions, S21)
- **LEGEND:** "Legend has it that 'Maids of Honour' were brought to notoriety by Henry VIII … he took the recipe and kept it under lock and key at Richmond Palace!" (S1)
- **REPORTED:** A Maids of Honour recipe appears in R. May's *The Accomplisht Cook*, 2nd edition, 1665 (S21).

### Richmond, before Kew
- **REPORTED (business):** "Certainly by the early 18th century the recipe had been disclosed to a bakery in Richmond and the tasty little tarts became one of the features of fashionable Richmond…" (S3)
- **REPORTED (business):** "The first Original Maids of Honour shop was on the corner of Hill Street in Richmond under the ownership of Mr John Billet…" (S3). The Council spells the name Billett, dates the family there from 1830, and dates the Hill Street shop from 1750 under Thomas Burdekin (S21).
- **VERIFIED (artefact):** A postcard dated "September 25th 1907" shows "THE ORIGINAL SHOP FOR MAIDS OF HONOUR" with Billett's name and the caption "ESTABLISHED IN RICHMOND NEARLY 200 YEARS". It is on the history page (S3).

### The Newens family and the Kew Road shop
- **VERIFIED (S3, S21 agree):** "Here a young lad called Robert Newens served an apprenticeship and went on to open his own premises, first in King Street and later at No 3 George Street…"
- **Business claim:** "in 1850, his son Alfred Nashbar Newens opened a brand new establishment on the Kew Road – exactly where we are today." (S3)
  - Our Story gives 1860 for the move to Kew (S2).
  - The Council gives 1887 for Alfred opening 288–290 Kew Road (S21).
  - The RTT article gives "founded in 1868" (S19).
  - **[TO CONFIRM]**
- **VERIFIED (photo):** "Alfred Newens and his family and staff posed in front of the Kew shop." (S1). The homepage dates it to the 1890s (S1); the blog title says "1800s" (S17).
- **VERIFIED (S17):** "Mrs Newens stands to the left of Alfred. On his right is his son Frank, who distinguished himself as a photographer and his daughter Kathleen who with John, (holding the pony) continued in business all her life."
- **VERIFIED:** "Alfred Newens died, leaving his business to his son John and daughter Kathleen in the 1920s." (S3; the Council gives 1927, S21)

### The Blitz and the rebuild
- **VERIFIED (S3, S21):** "during World War II (1939-1945), the elegant early Victorian building that housed the bakery, shop and dining room suffered severe bomb damage…"
- **REPORTED (S3):** "A surveyor's report from 1947 describes the site of the bakery as 'little more than a pile of rubble, the baker's oven a charred and blackened hulk at its centre'."
- **VERIFIED (S3, S21):** "John Newens' son Peter left the army and with his family, set work to get the business back on its feet. The bake house was rebuilt with new gas ovens installed, and the shop front was remodelled."
- **REPORTED (S3):** "…the new premises had a welcoming and homely appeal with its distinctive Mock Tudor mix of painted pebbledash, red clay roof tiles, heavy timbering and casement windows".
- **REPORTED (S3):** "Visitors today to the Original Maids of Honour are greeted by this very same shop front…"

### Today
- **VERIFIED (S1, S3):** "Today these delectable pastries are still served warm from our ovens…"
- **REPORTED (S3):** "And the Maids of Honour recipe? That's still a secret – one that's safe with us for a good long time to come…"
