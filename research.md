### What's wrong with it

- **Mobile hierarchy.** On a phone, the first screen is a raster logo, a postal address and then a full-height green list of eleven nav links. No food, no "book" action and no hours appear before you scroll through the whole menu tree.
- **Tone and trust.** The 2014 TripAdvisor badge and the "2001–2015" footer tell visitors the site is unloved, and that undercuts a restaurant that is clearly still busy (OpenTable lists 300+ reviews).
- **Missing essentials.** No opening hours anywhere. Prices and offers contradict each other between pages (10% vs 20%, £11.95 vs £18.95 lunch).
- **Typography.** Default fonts mixed across pages (the "Cancel reservation" link is in default blue Times), centred body copy at double line spacing, and justified footer text with wide gaps between words.
- **Booking.** Booking lives on a separate page, and the offer that rewards booking online is buried at the bottom.
- **The best story is hidden.** "The restaurant with the washing", the Naples look and 40-odd years in Richmond sit on an About page, not on the homepage.
- **Speed and technology.** An old PHP site with raster text images. I couldn't measure performance directly because the site blocks automated requests.

> **Fact to flag:** "about 25 years" comes from Time Out's 2013 listing and OpenTable. The restaurant's own About page dates it to **1983** (43 years), with Kew Road since **2006** (20 years). The concepts say "Richmond since 1983" marked [TO CONFIRM], and avoid "25 years".

## 2. Reference sites (principles only)

Each site was opened in headless Chromium at 1440px and 390px.

**Restaurants and hospitality**

1. **Bocca di Lupo (Soho).** Oxblood field, small wordmark, and the practical facts (lunch and pre-theatre menu, open till midnight) set as plain text in the first scroll. Menus are underlined uppercase text links, not PDFs. *Weakness:* on mobile, announcements and the cookie bar push the photo below the fold.
2. **The River Café (Hammersmith).** A handwritten wordmark on flat bright blue gives instant recognition with no photo. The reservations page states the booking rules honestly (table time by party size, phone in red for anything bigger).
3. **Lina Stores.** Illustrated shopfronts stand in for location photos, and the green awning stripe becomes the brand pattern. Illustration does real work as wayfinding, which is directly useful while we have no photography.
4. **Bancone.** A quiet serif wordmark, one plate, one sentence ("Fresh pasta made by hand every day"). Restraint as confidence. *Weakness:* a newsletter pop-up interrupts the first scroll.
5. **Padella.** The menu is typeset text on black with aligned prices: readable, fast, honest. *Weakness:* a group-bookings modal covers the page on arrival.
6. **Trullo (Highbury).** The hero is a time-of-day caption over a full-bleed image. The day itself becomes the structure. I'm taking the idea of time as an organising device, not its form.
7. **Dishoom.** Everything is written in character, down to the cookie banner. Proof that the copy voice is as much the brand as the type.
8. **Noble Rot.** The homepage is a magazine cover with three direct actions underneath. Editorial identity without losing the booking.
9. **Petersham Nurseries (Richmond neighbour).** Muted greens and a classic serif, aimed at the same Richmond and Kew visitor. *Weakness:* closures are announced in a modal that blocks everything.
10. **Maaemo (Oslo; Awwwards SOTD, Dec 2014).** Four corners: Information, Reservations, Gift Cards, Contact. Every action is visible without scrolling, an extreme reduction that still works ten years on.

**Awwwards winners and nominees**

- **Délice, Québec (Honorable Mention, Sep 2026).** A saturated red field, chunky expressive display type and food cut-outs inside organic shapes. A persistent bottom action bar (Visit, Book, Order, Gift card) keeps the transactional layer one tap away whatever the visuals are doing.
- **Tigermilk (Site of the Day, Jul 2023).** A two-colour system (#FCF3EA / #0D3026), illustration and type-led layouts, and a custom cursor. Playfulness carried by a strict palette.
- **Amici (Site of the Day and Developer Award, Oct 2024).** Olive-and-cream two-colour system with a mouse-trail hero and sticker-style details. The live site failed to load for me, so this note comes from the Awwwards case page only.
- **Studenterkilden, Copenhagen (Honorable Mention, Aug 2026).** A botanical illustrated emblem on generous white, with editorial serif headlines ("A historic house with settings for every occasion"). Heritage handled with lightness.
- **Lingers, South Tyrol (Honorable Mention, Aug 2026).** A giant wordmark set across the page as texture, and a booking date strip in the flow of the page rather than hidden behind a button.

**Anti-patterns seen repeatedly:** cookie banners, newsletter pop-ups and notice modals covering the first frame on mobile (six of ten sites); gold-gradient pill buttons; hours hidden in the footer.

## 3. How the best Italian and neighbourhood restaurants handle the basics

- **Menu:** HTML text, never a PDF or image. Grouped by course in Italian with English description lines. Prices right-aligned in tabular figures without "£.00" noise. One dietary key, shown once and repeated as small markers. Set menus (lunch, party) shown as their own short cards with the conditions written plainly.
- **Booking:** One "Book" action, reachable from every screen (usually top-right, plus a sticky bar on mobile). Party size, date and time first, then hand-off to the booking system. Phone for large groups and same-day bookings, stated with the reason. A cancellation route is visible.
- **Hours:** Stated as kitchen hours, by day, near the booking action, plus "open now / closes at" where the data is reliable. Exceptions (bank holidays, private events) shown inline, not in a modal.
- **Location:** A written address that can be copied, a tap-to-open map link (no heavy embedded map on load), the nearest station and walking time, and parking rules.
- **Allergens:** UK law requires information on the 14 major allergens for every dish. For non-prepacked food it may be given verbally, but there must be written signposting. The FSA's January 2025 best-practice guidance encourages written allergen information. Good sites carry a clear line: "Tell us about allergies when you book; ask for the allergen sheet."
- **Takeaway:** Named plainly ("Pizza to take away: call us") with a phone link; delivery platforms linked as a secondary option.

## 4. Principles I'll apply

1. **Book, menu, call and find-us are visible in the first frame at 390px**, and reachable from every scroll position. No pop-ups, no modals on arrival.
2. **The washing line is the brand.** It ran from "the restaurant with the washing" on Hill Street in 1983 [TO CONFIRM] to the line of shirts across today's dining room. It's the one story no other Richmond Italian can tell, so each concept uses it, but differently. The interior's own colours (deep-blue ceiling, white cloths, yellow napkins and roses) are real brand material too.
3. **Keep the existing equity:** the red script and the green are 40 years old. Each concept evolves them rather than discarding them.
4. **Menus are typeset text** with right-aligned tabular prices, Italian names and English descriptions, and a dietary key. Inferred markers and prices are flagged.
5. **Practical facts are written honestly**, including the conditions (offer exclusions, party-menu rules, parking after 4.30pm).
6. **No photography, so illustration and type do the work**, as Lina Stores and River Café prove. Image slots are clearly labelled with the shot to commission.
7. **One designed moment per concept**, built from the restaurant's own story, not a stock effect.
8. **Static-first:** each page is complete without JS; motion only enhances, and reduced-motion users get the finished frame.
