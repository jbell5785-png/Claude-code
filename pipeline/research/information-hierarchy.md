# Information hierarchy for restaurant sites: evidence, a "Useful first" standard and an audit rubric

Researched 26 October 2026 for the Richmond illustration-led concepts. It answers one question: how does a site with a strong signature interaction still give a diner what they came for, first?

**Source strength key**
- **[P]** Primary research with a stated method: usability or eyetracking studies, peer-reviewed papers, large surveys with a stated sample.
- **[I]** Industry or vendor survey. The method is partly stated, but the sponsor has a commercial interest.
- **[G]** Standards and guidance: W3C, GOV.UK and the FSA.
- **[W]** Weaker: secondary reporting, figures I could not trace to the primary document, or practitioner opinion.

Where a figure came from a search summary rather than a page I fetched, it is marked *(via secondary)*.

## (a) Evidence summary

### 1. What diners look for, and in what order

No public study ranks restaurant-website content items by importance. I searched NN/g directly: it has no restaurant-website study, only general homepage and navigation work. MeasuringU benchmarked chain *online ordering*, not information-seeking. The order below is therefore triangulated from surveys of what diners check and what puts them off.

**Working order of need on a phone.** This is an inference from the sources above.
1. **Is it open, and where is it?** The intent is immediate: about 30% go within the hour, "open now" searches are rising, and nearly half don't search until within an hour of going. Hours and address come first.
2. **What's on, and what does it cost?** 83–86% check the menu, and 41% of UK adults check it for prices.
3. **How do I get a table?** Book online or by phone. The phone is still the top channel (69%), and walk-in rules matter.
4. **Can I eat here?** Dietary and allergen information: 83% of UK adults expect it online, and FSA best practice says it should be available in writing before ordering.
5. **How do I get there?** Directions, transport, parking and step-free access. This rests only on the Yext attribute figure; I found **no survey that measures parking specifically**.
6. **What is it like?** Atmosphere, photos and story. 45% look for food photos, but this comes after the decision data, not before it.

### 5. Award-winning hospitality sites that balance both

Caveat: award juries mostly review desktop builds, and live sites change after the award. I checked the *live* sites in October 2026, so they may differ from the awarded versions.

1. **Dishoom.** Awwwards Site of the Day on 10 Jan 2015 (Clubhouse Studio): https://www.awwwards.com/sites/dishoom. On the live site, a rich illustrated, story-led brand ("a love letter to Bombay") sits *beneath* a header that always carries Menus, Cafés and a filled **Book a table** button. The first content block repeats two literal calls to action, *View menus* and *Book a table*, and every story section ends with the same two buttons. Hours live on each café page, one tap from the Cafés menu. **Pattern: the expressive content is the scroll; the actions follow you.**
2. **sketch, London.** Rebuilt by HATO in Sept 2018 with games and 3D avatars instead of photos (The Sybarite, 12 Sep 2018: https://thesybarite.co/sketch-london-unveils-new-interactive-online-experience). It is listed on CSS Design Awards (https://www.cssdesignawards.com/sites/sketch/33866/), but I could not confirm the award tier. The live site keeps **INFO, BOOK and RESERVATIONS & MENUS** as header toggles with the **address and phone** right there. Each room has hours plus *Menu* and *Book* buttons, and every game card carries **Book · Info · Play**, so play is one option among three, never a gate. **Pattern: the signature runs in parallel; essentials sit beside it, not behind it.**
3. **Tastavents, Badalona.** Awwwards Honorable Mention on 3 Oct 2024 (PAISANA Studio): https://www.awwwards.com/sites/tastavents-restaurant. Minimal and editorial, with numbered chapters (I–VI) as the navigation and a persistent **Reservas** button. The **hero itself carries a tap-to-call booking line and the address linked to maps**. Hours and booking rules are in chapter VI and the footer. The weakness is that the tasting menu is a **PDF**. **Pattern: chapter navigation as information scent; contact details in the hero.**
4. **Maaemo, Oslo.** Awwwards Site of the Day on 6 Dec 2014: https://maaemo.no. Radical restraint: a big wordmark and four literal links (*Information, Reservations, Gift cards, Contact*). The expression comes from type and imagery, not interaction. **Pattern: a confident brand doesn't need to gate anything.**
5. **Genesis, Shoreditch.** Awwwards Site of the Day and Developer Award, plus CSSDA Website of the Day, in Sept 2018 (Herdl: https://herdl.com/genesis-wins-sotd-developer-awards/). This is the closest analogue to our concepts: a one-page, black-and-white **animated SVG illustration scene** (Garden of Eden, with a burger as the forbidden fruit) that reacts to scroll and cursor. I **could not verify** where its menu, hours or ordering sat, because the live site returned 503. I include it as a reminder that illustration-led "scene" sites win awards on craft, and the award says nothing about usefulness.

**Common moves across 1–4:** literal labels for the top tasks (*Book, Menu, Info/Find us*); a booking action that persists; the address and phone placed in or beside the hero; the expressive layer running in parallel to the essentials; and expression carried mainly by illustration, type and voice rather than by gating interaction.

## (b) The "Useful first" standard for restaurant sites

Each item is a pass or fail requirement unless it says otherwise. "Essentials" means: **today's opening status, address/area, the primary action (Book, or Call where booking is by phone or walk-in only), a route to the menu, and the phone number.** For Richmond concepts, items marked "to confirm" in the copy deck still appear, with the "to confirm" marker.

### A. The first phone screen (390×844; design to a ~390×660 safe area for Safari toolbars)
1. **Name and plain-English descriptor** as real text: what it is and where. For example, "Neapolitan pizza on Richmond Hill". It must not be only a logo or illustration.
2. **Today's status**, as real text: "Open today 12–3 · 6–10:30", or "Closed today, open Tue 12:00". Show the full week's hours within one scroll (item 9).
3. **One primary action button** (Book a table, or Call to book) plus a **secondary Menu link**. Use literal labels, at least 44×44 px, in the lower half of the screen or in the action bar (item 26).
4. **Short address linked to maps**, e.g. "12 Hill Rise, TW10 · Directions". The phone number can sit here or in the action bar.
5. **Essentials render as HTML before any JS, font or SVG animation.** They are visible at first paint, with no loader, intro or splash in front of them. LCP is 2.5 s or less on a mid-range phone with 4G throttling.
6. **No essential is inside, behind or covered by the signature**, even mid-animation. Screenshot the first screen at 0 s, at 1 s and mid-signature: the essentials are legible in all three.
7. **No false floor.** Something from the next section peeks above the bottom edge, or the composition clearly continues.

### B. Within one to two scrolls (roughly the first 1,700 px at 390 wide)
8. **Menu taster with prices:** 3–6 real dishes with £ prices and dietary marks, plus "See the full menu". If the menu is a sample, it carries a visible sample label.
9. **Full opening hours table:** every day, including closed days, last orders or kitchen close, and seasonal notes. Days are written out, and times use one format.
10. **Find us:** full address, a map link (not an embedded map that loads before the essentials), the nearest station or bus, a parking note, and a step-free access note.
11. **Booking details:** how to book (online, phone or walk-in), group size limits, the deposit or cancellation policy if any, and the phone number again.
12. **Dietary line:** what they cater for (V, VG, GF) and how to ask about allergens, linking to the menu's allergen information.
13. **All of A and B work on the home page with JavaScript off** and with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

### C. The signature moment (scroll walk, interactive sign, unlockable box…)
14. **Parallel, never a gate.** Every essential can be reached without interacting with the signature, scrolling through it, or waiting for it. The signature is something you *can* do, never something you *must* get past.
15. **Placement:** on mobile, the signature starts at or below the essentials on the first screen, or it is the first screen's *backdrop* with the essentials laid over it as static text (see item 6). It never pushes the essentials below the first screen.
16. **Time budget:** zero delay to the essentials. Any automatic portion runs **3 s or less**, which keeps it well under WCAG's 5 s limit. Anything longer needs a visible pause control. UI micro-animations run 100–400 ms.
17. **Scroll budget:** native scroll speed and direction always, with **no scrolljacking**. Pinned or scrubbed sections are at most **1 screen height on mobile** (at most 1.5 on desktop) and never contain text the user needs to read. A visible "Skip to menu / booking" link sits before any pinned section.
18. **Hidden content is duplicated.** Anything revealed by the interaction (what's in the box, what the sign says, what each room is) is also available as plain text elsewhere. The interaction adds delight; it does not hold information.
19. **Plays once, then rests.** The final state stays when the user scrolls back up, and it does not replay on every pass.
20. **Reduced motion:** a designed static equivalent with the same information and the same essentials. This is not a broken or blank frame.
21. **Operable by everyone:** keyboard-reachable with visible focus; not hover-only; no drag, draw or tilt without a single-tap alternative; a sensible screen-reader reading order with the decorative SVG `aria-hidden`.
22. **Performance:** signature assets load after the essentials (deferred or lazy) and do not count against LCP. If the device is slow or JS fails, the page is still complete without them.

### D. Menu page
23. **HTML menu, not a PDF.** A PDF may be offered as a labelled, sized download ("Print version, PDF, 180 KB"), never as the only menu.
24. **A price on every priced item**, in £ with consistent formatting, plus a service charge statement if one applies.
25. **Course jump links at the top** (Starters · Mains · Sides · Puddings · Drinks), labelled "On this menu". They may stick as a compact chip row. There is a back-to-top link, and section headings are real `h2`/`h3` elements.
26. **Dietary marks with a legend** at the top: text abbreviations (V, VG, GF, N) or symbols, each with an accessible name. Add an allergen statement covering the 14 allergens and how to ask, per the FSA 2025 guidance. Name the specific nut or cereal where known.
27. **Currency of the menu:** a "Menu for [season/date]" or "changes daily" line. Unconfirmed menus carry the sample banner from the round-2 content rules.
28. **Scannable entries:** the dish name comes first and is bold, the description is at most about 15 words, the price is aligned, body text is at least 16 px, and there are no images of text. Long menus use headings and jump links rather than collapsed accordions.
29. **The menu page has its own essentials:** the booking action (bar or inline at the end), today's hours, and the address in the footer.

### E. Persistent action bar (every page, mobile)
30. **A bottom bar in the thumb zone with 2–3 literal actions:** **Book · Call · Find us**, or **Book · Menu · Find us** when the phone is in the header. Use words, not icons alone.
31. **Size and behaviour:** at most about 64 px plus the safe-area inset. It is opaque and has at least 4.5:1 contrast. It stays put or is partially persistent (300–400 ms slide-in). It is never covered by the signature or a cookie banner.
32. **It never hides focus or anchor targets.** Set `scroll-padding-bottom` to the bar's height, which meets WCAG 2.4.11.
33. **Desktop:** the same actions are visible in the header, with a Book button that is always visible.

### F. Labels, copy and consistency
34. **Literal labels lead.** Creative or voiced labels are welcome *alongside* plain ones ("The Larder, our menu"), never instead of them. Headings start with the key word: "Opening hours", not "When we're about".
35. **Every page has a footer** with the address, phone, hours summary and booking link.
36. **Facts match Google Business Profile and the booking platform.** Hours, phone and address are identical everywhere, and "to confirm" items are flagged rather than guessed.

## (c) Audit rubric (0–2 per item)

Test each concept at **390×844** in a mobile browser or device emulation with 4G throttling, then repeat with JS off, with reduced motion on, and with keyboard only.

**Scoring:** 0 = missing or blocked; 1 = present but delayed, buried or partial; 2 = meets the standard. The maximum is **32**.

**Pass rule:** a score of **0 on any gate item (G) fails the concept**, whatever its total. Otherwise, **26 or more** (about 80%) passes and 20–25 needs revision.

| # | Item | 0 | 1 | 2 | How to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (G) | **Essentials on the first screen** (status, primary action, menu route, address) | Two or more missing | One missing, or only reachable via the menu or bar | All four visible at rest | Screenshot at 390×660 and 390×844 |
| 2 (G) | **Time to essentials** | Hidden behind an intro, loader or signature | Visible but faded or animated in for more than 500 ms | Visible at first paint | Record a video of the load and step through frames |
| 3 (G) | **Signature is parallel, not a gate** | An essential is reachable only through the interaction | Reachable, but only by scrolling through the signature | Reachable directly via nav, bar or skip link | Try to book and to open the menu without touching the signature |
| 4 | **Signature budget** (≤3 s auto, ≤1 screen pinned on mobile, native scroll) | Scrolljacked, or pinned for more than 2 screens | Within 2× budget | Within budget | Count scroll gestures through it; time it with a stopwatch |
| 5 | **Hidden content duplicated** | Information only inside the interaction | Partly duplicated | Fully available as plain text | Read the page with the interaction never triggered |
| 6 (G) | **Reduced motion and no-JS** | Broken, blank or missing essentials | Works but is undesigned | A designed static equivalent with everything present | DevTools emulation of reduced motion; disable JS |
| 7 | **Hours table** | None | Partial (no closed days or last orders) | Full week within 2 scrolls | Find it within 2 scrolls |
| 8 | **Menu taster with prices** within 2 scrolls | None | No prices, or 3 scrolls or more | Priced dishes with dietary marks within 2 scrolls | Measure scroll depth |
| 9 | **Find us** (address, map link, transport, parking, access) | Address missing | Address only | All five | Check the text |
| 10 | **Booking clarity** (method, phone, policy, walk-ins) | Unclear how to book | Method only | Method, phone and policy | Check the text |
| 11 (G) | **Menu page is HTML** | PDF or image only | HTML but hard to scan (no headings) | HTML with headings | Open on the phone |
| 12 | **Menu navigation** (course jumps, back to top) | None | Headings only | Labelled jump links and back-to-top | Tap through on the phone |
| 13 | **Prices and dietary marks** | Missing | Partial, or symbols without a legend | Every item, with a legend and allergen statement | Check the text |
| 14 | **Persistent action bar** | None | Present but icon-only, covers content, or has too many items | 2–3 literal actions, opaque, scroll-padding set | Scroll the page; tab through it |
| 15 | **Accessibility of the interaction** (keyboard, focus, targets ≥24 px, no drag-only, pause control if >5 s) | Signature not operable by keyboard | Operable but with gaps | Fully operable | Keyboard-only run; check target sizes |
| 16 | **Literal labels and information scent** | Navigation is only creative labels | Mixed, with some vague labels | Literal labels lead, with voice alongside | Read the nav aloud: can a stranger predict each target? |

**Reporting format per concept:** total /32, any gate failures, the weakest three items, and one concrete fix for each. For example: "Move hours out of the sign's swing animation into static text under the shopfront; keep the sign as decoration."

## (d) Voice rule: the site talks to diners, never about itself
Added after client review, 27 Oct 2026. The concepts contained designer commentary, e.g. "This proposal is built on that wall. The step… moves down as you read", annotated architectural drawings, and captions like "drawn from photographs for this proposal. Not to scale."

1. **Write every word as it would appear on the restaurant's live site,** read by a diner deciding where to eat. Use the restaurant's voice (we/our) or a neutral voice, never the designer's.
2. **Never explain the design on the page.** No "this proposal", "this concept", "this page", "as you scroll", "each course is a slat", or why an interaction exists. The design must work without being explained.
3. **No annotated diagrams or callout labels.** Drawings carry atmosphere, not notation.
4. **Atmosphere in a sentence, not a paragraph.** Cut decor, joinery and planning history unless the business itself tells that story.
5. **Design rationale goes in NOTES.md** under "Pitch notes (not for the site)", where the designer uses it in the pitch.
6. **Before handing over, read the page's full visible text once as a diner** and cut anything that sounds like the designer talking.
