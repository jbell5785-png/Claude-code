# Readiness audit, 27 October 2026

**Question:** are the sites good enough to send to the businesses? Do they make sense, avoid generic fonts and layouts, and actually work?

**Answer: no, not yet.** All 15 things checked came back as *needs work*: the 12 concept sites, your portfolio and the three evidence sheets. None are broken beyond repair. Most of the problems are fixable in a day or two per business, but each site has at least one thing an owner would notice within a minute.

## How it was checked

- **Three separate reviewers per site:**
  - content and accuracy;
  - design originality and craft;
  - whether it works.
- **What they did:** read every word as a diner and as the owner, and fact-checked it against the research. They took screenshots at five screen sizes, with and without JavaScript and with reduced motion, and looked at them. They tapped every link and button, used each signature interaction by mouse, touch and keyboard, and tested the "open now" logic at faked times (early morning, late night, Sunday, Christmas Day, the clock change).
- **An independent verifier re-checked every finding.** The reviewers raised 1,238 raw findings. The verifier merged duplicates across reviewers and refuted 145 it couldn't reproduce or that were intentional.
- **What's left:** 460 verified issues: 23 blockers, 134 major and 303 minor or polish.

## 1. Fix before anything is sent (the pitch itself is wrong)

- **The Maids of Honour headline claim is false.** Their own Set Tea booking page says: *"for both bookings and walk-ins from 12:00 till 15:00 there is a minimum spend of £20 per person"*. I checked this myself against the live page. Google is repeating the tearoom's own words, not making a mistake.
  - The one-pager's lead row, its 10→18 score and the email subject line are all built on this.
  - The founding-history row has the same problem: Google is repeating their own Our Story page.
  - **Better angle:** "your own pages disagree (£20 on the page, £22 on the menu) and the rule sits on the tea page, which can put families off". That's true, and it's still useful to them.
- **Tide Tables' "Can I book? Wrong" row is unsupported.** The research leans the other way. Two Tide scores were measured against hours the sheet itself now contradicts.
- **The score panels on the sheets describe a search test that didn't happen as described.** Reword them to say what was actually done.
- **The emails overstate their own evidence sheets in places.** They need re-drafting once the sheets are corrected.
- **Your portfolio says the drawings are hand-drawn and that you illustrate them yourself.** They're generated by a script, so don't claim that. The same care applies to "original illustration" in the pricing guide and the pitches.

## 2. Problems shared by many sites (fix once, improves many)

1. **The preview banner breaks the first screen on 9 of the 12 published previews.** The "Speculative design concept" strip is inserted above sites with fixed headers. It covers the logo and header buttons, and on phones it pushes Book and Call under the bottom bar. It's a blocker on Mamma B, Matsuba A, Tide A and Tide B. The cause is one tool (`pipeline/tools/banner_build.py`), so one fix republishes them all. A slim strip that the layout accounts for will do it.
2. **The three concepts for a business look like one template.** Matsuba A and B open with the same headline, the same status line and the same buttons. Within Matsuba, Maids and Tide, the "today" card, the Visit block and the sample dishes are word for word the same across A, B and C. This is the generic feel you sensed. It came from retro-fitting the same useful-first blocks everywhere. **Fix:** keep the facts, but give each concept its own form for them. For example:
   - Tide A: hours chalked on the vault boards;
   - Tide B: an engraved plate under the bridge drawing;
   - Maids A: a painted fascia board with gilt Book plaques.
3. **Too many "to confirm" tags.** Caffè Mamma has 37–43 per site, with 3–5 on the first phone screen. Some dishes show a price tag that just says "Price", and there's an empty Christmas card. **Fix:** flag each fact once, delete stub lines that carry no fact, and add one footer line ("N details to confirm with you"). Better still, ring each place once and settle the hours before sending.
4. **Researcher voice is still on the pages:**
   - "Not published" rows on all three Tide concepts;
   - the owner's own copy quoted back in quotation marks, with their typos (Maids);
   - "Tide Tables doesn't publish a menu… Ours is…";
   - "DRAWN TIDE, ILLUSTRATIVE".
5. **Generic fonts.** All six Caffè Mamma fonts are fashionable stock picks: Young Serif, Instrument Sans, Italiana, Hanken Grotesk, Bricolage Grotesque and Atkinson Hyperlegible. Tide C's Unbounded with Onest reads as tech or SaaS. Caffè Mamma's real red brush-script logo appears nowhere, and that's the single biggest reason those three feel generic.
6. **Small phones were never tested.** At 320–360px, Matsuba A and C clip text and the Matsuba A action bar falls off screen. Maids A and Tide B overflow too. Our QA only tested 390px and up.
7. **The booking forms don't book.** On all three Caffè Mamma concepts and on Matsuba A, the form asks for party size, date and time, then opens the booking site with none of them filled in. It also offers times that have already passed. **Fix:** pass the values through, or replace the form with a plain "Book on OpenTable" button.
8. **Illustration craft lets several signatures down:**
   - Tide A's keystone face reads as clip-art;
   - both Maids tarts look like a vol-au-vent or a pancake stack;
   - the Caffè Mamma shirts are one reused icon shape;
   - Tide's plane tree and garden drawings are weak.
9. **Below the hero, most sites fall back to a stock section stack:** text on colour blocks, a marquee, a giant footer wordmark. Only a few carry their idea all the way down the page.
10. **Leftover placeholders:** "Photograph to come" frames on Matsuba B and C, and "Portrait to come" on the portfolio.

## 5. Suggested order of work

1. **Correct the claims** on the evidence sheets and in the emails (a few hours). Ring Matsuba, Maids and Tide once each to settle the disputed hours; that alone removes most of the tags.
2. **Shared fixes:** the banner tool, small-phone overflow, booking forms, voice leftovers and tag clean-up (about a day for all 12).
3. **Each business's blockers and majors,** lead concept first (about a day per business).
4. **De-template each business's three concepts,** with its own form for today and visit info, and retype Caffè Mamma (1–2 days per business).
5. **Illustration upgrades on the lead concepts** (the keystone face, the tarts, the shirts, Tide's garden).
6. **Portfolio:** honest wording, the mobile bug, real screenshots of the work, and your own domain.

Sending only the lead concept per business, fixed properly, is a reasonable shortcut if you want to start outreach sooner.
